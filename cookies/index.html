The short version

This site sets no cookies at all unless you accept them. If you decline, or simply ignore the banner, nothing is stored and nothing about your visit is measured. Accepting turns on two analytics tools, listed in full below.

There is no advertising on this site, no retargeting, and no third party is paying us to place anything in your browser. The only reason we would like to measure anything is to find out which pages are worth writing more of.

One thing is stored before you choose, and it is not a cookie, so it does not require consent. Your answer to the banner is kept in your browser's local storage so that we do not ask again on every page. It contains one word, either granted or denied, and it never leaves your device.

What a cookie is

A cookie is a small file a website asks your browser to keep. A first-party cookie is set by the site you are actually visiting. A third-party cookie is set by somebody else whose code is running on that page, and it can usually recognise you across other sites too.

That distinction matters here, so the tables below say which is which rather than lumping them together.

Analytics cookies

Google Analytics 4 tells us how many people visit, which pages they read and roughly where in the world they are. It sets two cookies, both first-party, and only after you accept.

Google Analytics 4 cookies set by this site
Name Type Expires Purpose
_ga First-party 2 years Distinguishes one visitor from another.
_ga_RTWEXCM20Y First-party 2 years Remembers where you are in a single visit.

Browsers shorten these on their own if you do not come back. Chrome caps them at 400 days and Safari at 7.

We also record a few specific actions: clicking the email address, clicking through to our Fiverr profile or our portfolio, reading a question on an FAQ page, and how far down a service page you read. None of these carry your email address or anything that identifies you personally. The email link in particular is recorded as a click and nothing more.

Session recording cookies

Microsoft Clarity records how pages are used, so we can see where a layout confuses people. It captures mouse movement, clicks and scrolling. It does not capture what you type, and this site has no forms, no login and no payment fields for it to capture in the first place.

Clarity is the heavier of the two and we treat it that way. It is not loaded onto the page at all unless you accept. When it is loaded, we tell it explicitly that it may use analytics storage and may not use advertising storage, because this site runs no advertising.

That distinction matters for what follows. Microsoft documents seven cookies for Clarity. Two are first-party and are the ones this site relies on. The other five are Microsoft's own, third-party, and are associated with advertising and cross-site identification. Denying advertising storage is intended to prevent those five being set.

How long Clarity keeps it

These periods are set by Microsoft rather than by us. We cannot extend them and we do not shorten them.

Microsoft Clarity data retention periods
Data Kept for
Session recordings (playback data) 30 days
Click and aggregated page data 9 months
Sessions we label or favourite 9 months
Heatmaps 9 months

Microsoft states that data on its servers, including backups, is deleted after these periods and cannot be recovered. Figures verified against Microsoft's published retention documentation on 17 August 2026, and worth re-checking before publication because they have changed before.

Changing your mind

Declining is not a temporary state you have to keep re-confirming. It is remembered, and no analytics cookie is written while it stands. Accepting can be reversed the same way.

[CONSENT WITHDRAWAL MECHANISM: confirm whether a persistent "Cookie settings" link is added to the footer. Recommended.]

Clearing this site's data in your browser also clears the stored choice, and the banner will simply ask again.

Controlling cookies yourself

Every browser lets you block or delete cookies without asking us first, usually under privacy or site settings. Blocking them all will not break anything here, because nothing on this site needs a cookie to work. There is no basket, no login and no saved state to lose.

What we do with personal data more broadly is set out in the privacy policy.
