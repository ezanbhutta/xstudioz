What a brand identity actually is

A brand identity is the complete designed system a company presents itself with. That means the logo and its lockups, colour, typography, layout and grid, imagery direction and tone of voice, plus the rules that say how those parts work together. The logo is one component of it, not the whole.

The word gets used loosely, so it is worth being exact. A mood board records a feeling without deciding anything, which leaves it short of being an identity. The brand is different again: it is what other people believe about you.

An identity removes decisions. Once it exists, the person laying out a pitch deck at eleven at night uses the grey the system already names, and never has to stop and choose one. That is the whole mechanism, and the line between a logo and an identity is worth understanding before you commission either.

What the system contains

Six things, plus the rules binding them: logo and lockups, colour, typography, layout and grid, imagery, and tone of voice. Each one is a set of decisions made once, in writing, so that nobody has to make them again under pressure.

The components of a brand identity system and what each one decides
Component What it fixes If it is missing
Logo & lockups The mark, its variants, clearspace and minimum size. Every placement is improvised, and the mark ends up stretched or crowded.
Colour A ground, a primary and supporting values, each in hex, RGB and CMYK, plus which pairings are allowed. Six near-identical blues appear within a year, and print never matches screen.
Typography Typefaces, weights, a size scale, and sensible line lengths for headings, body and captions. Each new page invents its own hierarchy from scratch.
Layout & grid Columns, margins, spacing steps, and where the mark sits on a page. Nothing lines up across two pieces made a month apart.
Imagery What photography, illustration or graphic language belongs here. And what does not. Stock pictures drift in and set the tone by accident.
Tone of voice How the company writes: register, vocabulary, and the things it never says. The brand looks like one company and reads like three.
The rules The document that binds all of the above and can be handed to someone new. The system exists only in the head of whoever designed it.

That last row is a capability in its own right. When the document has to be long enough for a team, a printer and a future agency to work from unaided, brand guidelines are a separate piece of work.

Why a system beats a folder of assets

A folder of assets answers the questions you had on the day it was made. A system answers the ones you have not had yet, because rules agreed in advance settle every new asset. Whoever happens to be making it does not have to decide.

Recognition is built by repetition, and repetition only happens if the same decisions keep getting made. Assets without a system stay consistent for as long as the original project is remembered; after that, whatever is most recent in the drive becomes the standard, and the drift compounds.

A system also makes work faster. A landing page or an investor deck does not have to be invented; it is assembled from parts that already agree. Our own site is the worked example. How the work runs

The same four stages as every project: brief, directions, refinement, delivery. What changes is what gets presented. A direction for an identity is the system shown doing real work, on the surfaces you publish on, with an argument for why it holds. A swatch on its own is not a direction.

If we drew the mark, the geometry is already decided and the system grows out of it. If you bring one, we read it first and build outward from what is there. Each stage is described in full on the process page.

Whether you need one yet

This is a good fit if:

  • You are publishing in several places at once: site, decks, packaging, social. Each one looks slightly different.
  • More than one person makes things, and you want them working from rules instead of instinct.
  • You are raising, hiring or entering a new market, and looking established is doing real work for you.

It is a poor fit if:

  • The company is still changing shape monthly. Build the system when there is something settled to build it around.
  • You need one mark for a small number of places. Commission the logo on its own and add the system when the surfaces multiply.
  • You want a template kit with your colours swapped in. That market is well served, and we are not competing in it.

We would rather say that up front than take a project that was never going to earn its cost. If you are unsure, read about who you would be working with, or write and describe the situation.

Common questions

What’s included in a brand identity package?

The logo and its lockups, a colour system with values for screen and print, a typographic scale, layout and grid rules, direction for imagery, guidance on tone of voice, and a written document saying how they combine.

A package is a system with rules, so the file count varies. Scope is agreed per project.

Is a logo enough, or do I need a full brand identity?

A logo is enough while the number of places your brand appears is small enough to hold in your head.

Once two people are making things at once, those decisions need to live outside a memory. That is where an identity starts earning its cost.

What’s the difference between brand identity and branding?

Brand identity is the designed system: marks, colour, type, layout and language, and the rules binding them. Branding is the ongoing work of building a reputation with it.

Identity is what a studio makes. Branding is what you do with it afterwards, for years. The brand itself is neither of those. It is the impression left in someone else’s head.

Should a startup invest in branding before launch or after?

The useful test is whether the company has stopped changing shape. The launch date on its own tells you little. If the product, the audience or the name is still moving month to month, a full identity will be rebuilt before it is used.

Once those settle, doing the work before launch is cheaper than doing it after, because afterwards every change means replacing something already public. The reasons the industry range is so wide are worth reading before you budget.

Can you build an identity around a logo I already have?

Often, yes. We read the existing mark closely first: its geometry, its weight, how it behaves small and in one colour. Then we build out of what is there.

If the mark cannot carry a system, we say so at the start and explain why. Building a system that quietly fights the mark helps nobody.
